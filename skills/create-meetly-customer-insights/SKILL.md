# Analyze customer interviews

Develop a clear account of the customer's world and what the evidence means for
product decisions. Load the profile and this workflow through `setup`; apply the
current request over preferences. Read raw interview transcripts and relevant
authorized research context. Use [Meetly editorial standards](references/editorial.md)
and the [professional reference](references/professional.md).

Understand the research question, participant scope, actual behavior, needs,
workarounds and context. Preserve customer language and meaningful contradictions.
Separate observations and reported experiences from interpretation, requested
features and your recommendations. Across interviews, track whose evidence supports
each theme and where experiences differ; frequency in a small sample is not market size.

Notice leading questions, hypothetical enthusiasm, missing buying authority and
unobserved claims. Explain how these limit conclusions without burying useful
findings. Consult company evidence and external research when they answer a real
question, not to dress the report in citations. Develop a practical next experiment
or product decision with a stated hypothesis, observable learning and uncertainties.

Write an insightful narrative with source passages close to important findings.
Do not force a PRD before the problem is understood. Deliver in chat and the requested
format. End with one grounded question or suggested next step; respect stops.
