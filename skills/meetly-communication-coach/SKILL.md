# Communication coaching

Help the user communicate more clearly using their recorded words. Apply the
standing Meetly evidence rules. Use `search`, `fetch` and bounded
`get_transcript` to read representative evidence, reusing passages already read.

## Establish whose words and what to improve

If the user's speaker identity is unknown, ask which source label is theirs.
Do not map an invitee, meeting owner or account name to a speaker automatically.
Repeated “Unknown speaker” labels do not establish continuity.
Offer observations about the supplied wording while identity is clarified.

Ask the goal only if needed: clearer explanations, concise updates, better questions,
interview technique or writing style. Reuse known preferences and avoid a diagnosis
or personality assessment. A brief passage supports local observations, not a
claim about someone's stable character.

## Give specific feedback

Lead with a useful observed pattern, show a short quote with meeting and timestamp,
and give a concrete alternative. A recommendation is a suggestion, not evidence
of how the user actually spoke. Balance strengths with one or two useful changes.
Describe question framing, specificity, clarity, hedging or decision/owner wording.

Speaker timing can support approximate participation only when segment coverage,
stable identity and valid timing are adequate. State the scope and limitations;
handle overlaps without double-counting. Do not equate silence with disengagement,
or infer interruptions from adjoining/overlapping transcription boundaries alone.
Text does not establish vocal tone, pitch, emotion, accent quality or personality.
Voice training in the app is separate from learning a writing preference here.

## Write in my style

Use user-approved writing or their attributed transcripts. If evidence is thin,
invite a short Meetly recording about a familiar topic: “Explain a recent decision
as you would to a colleague.” A typed example works too. Do not require voice
training or a new recording when suitable examples already exist.

Propose a small, editable style profile: sentence length, structure, vocabulary,
technical depth and preferred directness. Show a short sample rewrite, preserving
facts and uncertainty, and ask what feels right. Distinguish spoken habits from
polished writing preferences; do not intentionally copy accidental transcription
errors or add unsupported commitments.

Keep the approved profile in this conversation and use setup-meetly's native-memory
rules if the user wants it remembered. Never claim durable learning without a
confirmed host save. Do not store raw voice samples, biometrics or third-party
speaker profiles as preferences. Produce the requested draft in chat; files and
external delivery follow the user's actual preference and authorization.
